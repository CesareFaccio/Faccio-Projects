Air runs bottom to top. The shape is pinned at the cross and free to turn.
Controls

This is the air's viscosity, and so the Reynolds number. Thick air on the left: the flow stays attached and the wake closes quietly behind the shape. Thin air on the right: the flow separates, and the wake broadens and starts shedding vortices off alternate sides, which buffets the shape instead of letting it sit still.

Where the shape is pinned along its own long axis. Held near its nose it weathervanes, turning to lie along the flow, the way a dart or a weathervane does. Pinned at its centre it does the opposite and settles across the flow — that is the real physics, and the reason a dropped card lands flat rather than knifing down.
